# Strictly Medical: notes for working on this repo

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step. Hosted on GitHub Pages from `main` at the repo root.

## Layout
- `index.html`: the page. Loads the scripts below in order as classic scripts that share one global scope (no modules, no bundler). Order matters only for code that runs at load time.
- `css/style.css`: page and dialogue-panel styles.
- `js/sprites.js`: size and anchor (`w`, `h`, `ax`, `ay`, in game pixels) for each image in `assets/sprites/`.
- `js/core.js`: canvas setup, sprite loading (`art()`, `drawArt()`), math helpers.
- `js/data.js`: classes, failure rules, wounded knights, enemy types, and `S`, the saved game state.
- `js/world.js`: scene object `SC`, heights and stepping rules (`setHeights`, `canStep`), village and dungeon builders, party creation, grid movement, BFS pathfinding. Both layouts are loaded from generated map files (below). Buildings are listed in `SC.buildings`; each has `door`, the tile just outside its front (south-west) door, ready for enterable interiors later. `SC.gate` marks the Barrow gate. A building fades as a whole when it is drawn over the player (`buildingHides` in render.js).
- `js/map-dawnspire.js`, `js/map-spire.js` (generated, `MAPS.dawnspire` / `MAPS.spire`): the village island (78x78, Dawnspire Isle) and the first dungeon floor (78x78, the Ruptured Spire). Built by `art/maps/overworld.py` and `art/maps/dungeon.py` from the reference painting `art/tiles2/maps-reference.png`: the tile grid is projected onto the painting and each tile is classified from the pixels there (sky/abyss, grass, path, water, floor); terrace levels, stairs, buildings, landmarks, rooms, enemies and loot are hand-marked in image coordinates in those scripts, and stairs/corridors are added automatically until every walkable tile is reachable. Edit the scripts and rerun them rather than editing the map files.
- `js/tiles2.js` + `assets/sprites/tiles2.png` (generated by `art/tiles2/cut2.py` from `art/tiles2/tileset-sheet.png`): ground tops warped to the 64x32 diamond, flattened cliff/brick face strips, and props (trees, rocks, bushes, wheat, fences, windmill, towers, stalls, dungeon pillars, altars, fountains...). `drawT2()`, `drawTop()` in core.js.
- `js/input.js`: keyboard, mouse and touch. On touch screens, movement is either the isometric D-pad (a plus-shaped pad rotated 45° so each arm is a grid direction; the default) or the left-half joystick, set in Options (`OPTS.pad`). Action buttons sit in a thumb arc around Strike.
- `js/combat.js`: damage, attacks, projectiles, telegraphed attack tiles, medic actions (stabilize, carry, deliver).
- `js/actors.js`: player control, companion AI, enemies, the Lantern Warden.
- `js/ui.js`: end of run, dialogue panels, shops, quests.
- `js/title.js`: title screen (menu, options, credits, ember particles over the backdrop's braziers) and the pause menu (pause button, Esc or P). Options persist in localStorage under `sm-options` and are shared by the title and pause menus. Any open panel pauses the game loop.
- `js/render.js`: all drawing and the HUD. The HUD is Souls-style: unboxed health/stamina/skill bars top left with a lingering damage trail, purse top right under the pause button, boss bar along the bottom.
- `js/main.js`: game loop and start-up.
- `assets/sprites/`: game-ready sprites, already scaled for the 64x32 tile grid.
- Character sprite sheets (`CHARS` in `js/core.js`): a grid of frames at 3x game size. A unit's `look.sprite` picks the sheet; units without one are drawn procedurally in `drawFigure`. So far only the knight class has one (`knight.png`, pixel art from SpriteCook): four rows of 8 frames, walk front, walk back, sword attack front, sword attack back. Front frames face down-right and back frames up-right; they are mirrored for the other two directions, and the first walk frame doubles as the standing pose. A swing plays the attack row once over `atkDur`. Sources and SpriteCook asset IDs are in `art/characters/knight/pixel/` (`build.py` rebuilds the sheet); `art/characters/knight/` also holds the older ChatGPT sheet and an unused HD SpriteCook attempt.
- Village buildings are painted sprites (`BLD` in `js/core.js`, `assets/sprites/bld-*.png`), cut from `art/buildings/village-set.png` by `art/buildings/cut.py` and fitted to their footprints by `art/buildings/pack.py`, which also corrects the generated art's flatter viewing angle to the game's 2:1 grid (per-half horizontal scale plus a vertical column shear, so walls stay vertical). Each sprite is split at its front corner: the left half is drawn at the building's left-front tile and the right half at its right-back tile, so characters in front of either wall draw over it. `art/buildings/` also holds two unused chapel designs.
- `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `assets/icons/`: makes the game installable to a home screen and playable offline. Bump `VERSION` in `sw.js` when adding or renaming files.
- `assets/ui/`: title backdrop and emblem, compressed from the originals in `art/`.
- `art/`: source art (`emblem.png` is the logo and app-icon master; `title-bg.png` and `title-mockup.png` are the title screen backdrop and its reference mockup) (full-size tile cut-outs, the original ChatGPT tile sheet, SpriteCook character sprites). Not loaded by the game.

## Conventions
- Grid coordinates are tiles. `isoF(x,y)` maps to screen as `[(x-y)*32, (x+y)*16]`; `iso(x,y,z)` also lifts by height (`z`, or the ground height of the tile under the point). Movement is 4-directional along the grid in real time.
- Height: each tile has a level `SC.HT` (0-3) and ground height `SC.HZ` in px (`ZL`=40 px per level). Stairs are tiles with `SC.SD` = uphill direction; they sit half a level up and can only be walked along that direction. `canStep(a,b)` is the one movement rule (used by stepping, dashing, knockback and BFS). Walls (`T=2`) are tall tiles. Units interpolate height while moving (`pos(u).z`).
- Draw order: one diagonal sweep by `x+y`. Each tile draws its top plus textured faces down to any lower neighbour in front (or into the void at the world edge). Tiles not raised above the tiles behind them (`SC.EARLY`) are drawn one step early so they never clip units standing behind; raised tiles draw in place so they hide what is behind them. Attack warnings and other tile overlays are drawn right after their tile.
- Every enemy and boss attack telegraphs the exact tiles it will hit before it lands.
- World scale: characters are about 60px tall. `FIG`, `BS` and `PROP` in `js/core.js` scale code-drawn figures, buildings and village props up from their original sizes to match.
- Sprites come from generated art. Tile art from ChatGPT is about 1.6:1, so tiles are squashed to 2:1 when packed into `assets/sprites/`.

## Design decisions so far
- Title: Strictly Medical. The guild is the Order of the Ninety and Nine. Running gag: the guild insists everything it kills was self-defense.
- 2 to 4 player co-op is the goal; companions are AI for now. Online play is planned (WebRTC peer-to-peer works on GitHub Pages).
- Failure rule is undecided; Mercy, Standard and Grim are all selectable for testing.
- Being considered: every player is a knight (four distinct knight designs), and class comes from the equipped weapon.
- Character art direction: pale white/silver holy knights in a detailed, solemn HD pixel style.
- Don't spend SpriteCook credits unless the owner asks.
